Automation brief

The "why", taken from the case file.

Pain

About 60 notes captured in eight months, and none became posts. 40 abandoned drafts sit in Google Drive. The posts she does finish take 90 minutes to 3 hours each, and most of that is stalling, not writing. She hasn't posted in eleven weeks.

User

Meera Pillai, founder of Skinstinct (D2C skincare, Mumbai, ₹14-16 lakh a month in revenue). She has a pharma formulation background and writes for 28-40 year old urban women who are tired of being sold to. She has 6,200 LinkedIn followers.

Outcome

Three LinkedIn posts a week without it eating her time. Every draft is ready to review, sounds like her, and makes no claim she can't stand behind.

Journey today

Note goes into Telegram → forgotten → she opens a doc weeks later, writes two lines, decides it isn't good enough and closes it. A hired writer produced clean copy she spent longer rewriting than writing herself.

"I open the doc, I write two lines, I decide it's not good enough, I close it. By the time I come back to it two weeks later, the moment has passed."

Why it's worth doing: her four published posts earned 47,000 impressions. One post brought 340 profile visits in 48 hours and three wholesale enquiries.

Components map

Trigger → Input → Context → Processing → AI → Output.

Trigger

A new post in her notes channel (or a DM to the bot). A note worth a post is drafted within about a minute. A daily run also sweeps the backlog on Mon, Wed and Fri.

Input

The raw note: an observation, a customer DM reaction, a 2-liner, a pasted voice transcript, or a bulk .txt backlog.

Context

voice-skill.txt (rules, signature moves, published facts) plus all 15 of her pieces in full: 4 LinkedIn posts and 11 newsletters.

Processing

Stores and de-duplicates every note, pulls 3-6 keywords from it, ranks the backlog, varies topics, and runs a rule-based voice check (contractions, copying, hype words, length).

AI

Gemini scores the note (0-10, one-line reason) and pulls 3-5 keywords and a search phrase. The top Google News result for that phrase (no key needed) goes to Gemini with the note and the voice skill to write the draft.

Output

Score below 6: a one-line reason and no draft. Score 6+: the draft, the keywords and the news source, with a [VERIFY NEWS] flag if news was used, plus Approve / Redraft / Later / Skip. Human review gate: she posts it herself.

Parameters

Every setting is written down so it can be defended and changed.

1. Is this note worth a post? Scored 0-10

CriterionPointsWhat earns them
Specificity0-3A concrete scene, number, mechanism or observation, not a mood or a slogan
Expertise edge0-3Something a formulator or founder knows that her readers don't
Reader relevance0-2Useful to urban Indian women aged 28-40 who distrust skincare marketing
Writable without invention0-2Can be written from the note plus her published facts alone

2. Verdict thresholds

ScoreExamplesWhat happens
6-10A specific batch, test, customer question or number, with an idea a reader learns fromDrafted immediately and sent for review
4-5A real topic, but generic - no detail from her own workNo draft; one-line reason sent back. /draft <id> overrides
0-3Task reminders, logistics, moods, abandoned half-thoughts, sales pushes, any hard rejectNo draft; one-line reason sent back

Hard rejects

  • Names or identifies a customer, employee or supplier contact
  • Details of the two unreleased products
  • Attacks a named competitor
  • Needs a medical claim or a diagnosis
  • Sales pitch, discount, or a mood with no idea in it
  • Confidential commercial terms (pricing, margins, contracts)

Cadence and workload

  • A note scoring 6 or more is drafted straight away; below 6 gets a one-line reason and no draft
  • Mon, Wed, Fri mornings (IST): the best note still waiting in the backlog is drafted, so she gets 3 a week even from old notes
  • On demand at any time with /draft
  • No new draft while 3 are still waiting for review
  • Rotates topics: skips a pillar just used if another note is within 1 point
  • Bulk imports are scored 8 at a time; the rest are scored on the next daily run

The Cut

Meera stays the author of everything published

Automated: capture, scoring, drafting, news research. Stays human: review, edit, publish.

The insight isn't "don't automate". It's: automate the workflow without automating away the human's consequential judgment. So the bot never posts, schedules or auto-publishes to LinkedIn. Every draft stops at the human review gate: Meera approves and publishes it herself. The news angle comes with its source link so she checks it before it goes out under her name.
