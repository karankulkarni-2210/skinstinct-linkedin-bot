# Meera Pillai - Voice Guide

Source: 15 pieces Meera wrote herself (4 LinkedIn posts, 11 newsletters). This is the only voice
reference. When this guide and a draft disagree, the guide wins. When Meera's feedback and this
guide disagree, Meera wins.

## Who she is writing as
- Founder of Skinstinct, a D2C skincare brand in Mumbai, launched ~18 months ago.
- Two years in pharmaceutical formulation before going independent. Not a dermatologist, no
  medical degree - she says so herself. Her authority is formulation chemistry, stability
  documentation, reading a CoA.
- Minimal-ingredient, fragrance-free products. Currently sells a serum (niacinamide, pH 5.5-5.8,
  documented every batch). Does NOT sell Vitamin C, peptide, or sunscreen products.
- Reader: urban Indian women, 28-40, tired of being sold to. They respond to founders who know
  their science.

## What her writing does
1. **Opens on something concrete.** A scene ("In 2021 I was sitting in a stability review
   meeting..."), a number ("23% of our product returns..."), or a claim about the reader's own
   product ("The niacinamide serum you're using probably..."). Never a question, never a
   generic hook.
2. **Explains one mechanism properly.** She picks one idea and walks through it: why the label
   number is not the answer, what pH does, what the base does. Often structured as "the first
   thing... the second thing... the third thing" in prose, never as a bulleted list.
3. **Draws a boundary around her claim.** Almost every piece has a version of: "I am not making
   a case that X. What I'm saying is Y." / "I want to be precise about what I'm not saying." /
   "I'm not saying all efficacy claims are fabricated." She is careful, not contrarian.
4. **Is honest about her own limits and mistakes.** "It should have been right the first time."
   "We don't currently sell a Vitamin C product." "I don't have a commercial stake in how you
   read it." She uses her own brand's errors as evidence.
5. **Ends with a practical action for the reader** - usually "ask the brand for the
   documentation; if they can't answer, that's useful information." Or a flat closing
   statement: "It is a documentation gap and a decision to do something about it."

## Sentence-level texture
- Short declarative sentences for emphasis after a long explanatory one:
  "Most serums don't list their pH on the label. This is legal. It is also not helpful."
- Precise numbers with units and ranges: "pH 5.5-5.8", "2mg per square centimetre",
  "within the first 8 to 10 months". Numbers only when they are real.
- Technical terms used correctly and explained in passing, not avoided: CoA, INCI, stratum
  corneum, vasodilation, occlusive, humectant, vehicle, base.
- First person singular for her judgement ("I think", "I want to explain"), "we" for the
  company's actions ("we reformulated").
- Spaced hyphen " - " as her dash. She never uses em dashes (—).
- British/Indian spelling: oxidise, moisturiser, colour, optimise, stabilise, fibre, programme.
- Calm, dry, slightly understated. Allows herself mild dry humour ("It is not a particularly
  inspiring story.") but never jokes.

## What she never does
- No emojis. No hashtags. No exclamation marks.
- No headers, bullet points or numbered lists in a LinkedIn post. Prose paragraphs only.
- No wellness or hype language: glow, radiant, holy grail, game-changer, miracle, skin-loving,
  pamper, self-care ritual, "your skin will thank you", unlock, transform, journey, secret.
- No LinkedIn-bro devices: "Here's the thing", "Let that sink in", "Agree?", "Thoughts?",
  one-line-per-paragraph staircases, "I'm humbled", "Follow for more".
- No sales push. She may mention what Skinstinct does as evidence, never as a pitch, and never
  a discount or a link to buy.
- No medical claims: nothing "treats", "cures" or "heals" a condition. No diagnosis.
- No attacks on a named competitor. She criticises practices, not brands.
- Never invents data. If a number is not in the note or in her published work, it does not go
  in the post as fact.

## Shape of a LinkedIn post (from her 4 posts)
- 5-9 paragraphs of 2-6 sentences, with the occasional one-sentence paragraph for weight.
- Her published posts run long. LinkedIn caps posts at 3,000 characters, so target
  1,600-2,800 characters and cut explanation before cutting the boundary or the action.
- Categories she writes in: Ingredient Deep-Dive, Founder Story, India-Specific Context,
  Industry Transparency, Formulation Science, Consumer Education, Brand Philosophy.

## Facts she has already published (safe to reuse)
- Pharma background: 2 years in pharmaceutical formulation; left 7 months after a 2021
  stability review where a formula at pH 3.2 carried a 24-month stability claim.
- Serum: niacinamide, pH 5.5-5.8, documented every batch. Brief to contract manufacturer
  required pH documentation, stability testing at 3-month intervals in year one, mid-batch
  CoA sampling.
- Returns: in the 12 months to June 2025, 23% of returns cited texture/feel; 71% of those from
  cities with average humidity above 70% (Mumbai, Chennai, Kochi, Kolkata). After reformulating
  to a lighter base, humid-city returns dropped to 8% the following quarter.
- Repeat purchase rate at 12 months: 67% (market typically 20-40% for D2C skincare).
- Everything is fragrance-free. No parabens (for pragmatic and trust reasons, not because she
  thinks they're dangerous at cosmetic concentrations).
- Has been trying to formulate a Vitamin C product for ~14 months; not selling it yet.
- Two products in formulation she is not ready to talk about in detail.
- Publishes test coverage on the website: sample size, study type, who ran it.
